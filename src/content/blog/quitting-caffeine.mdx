I quit caffeine!

Not in one big "quitting" moment, but rather in a gradual series of tiny habit changes, over the course of a year.

Three sections in this blog post:

1. **Why** did I want to reduce my caffeine intake?
2. How I did this, via micro **habit changes**. Including **iced latte recipes**.
3. How to apply this to **yourself**.

# Why quit?

## Debilitating headaches

Last Fall I was having a LOT of migraines -- more than I had ever had before. These were combination tension headache / migraine headaches. I would have tension headache symptoms, like muscle tension in a "band" shape around my head AND migraine symptoms like nausea, flashing lights, and light sensitivity.

## Posture issues

I was also having a lot of posture issues. Really tight muscles.

Every day where I sat at a computer for more than a few hours I would feel VERY fatigued. It was hard to be at the computer for the whole day. I tried standing, sitting, laying down - but no matter what, the day of work was fatiguing.

I was doing physical therapy, and that helped a lot! But not completely.

And I was constantly changing the ergonomics of my setup - standing desk, walking desk, yoga ball, different chairs. But not completely.

SOMETHING was keeping my muscles perpetually tight throughout all of this (hint: it's the topic of this blog post).

## Energy issues

I would have a lot of energy during the day at work -- but when it came time to cook dinner or do evening activities, I was EXHAUSTED.

It felt like caffeine was concentrating it in the daytime work hours, stealing energy from the rest of my life.

I wished I could have a more steady and stable energy level, all day.


## Social issues

I was having social problems with caffeine, too.

A few times a month I'd arrive late to an event because I HAD to pick up a coffee on the way. And I mean, often I did have to -- or else I'd get a bad headache. I could feel it coming

I really like to arrive punctually and reliably, so this stressed me out to be inconsistent!


# How did I quit?

"Quit" is actually the wrong word for my approach. It wasn't a one-and-done moment at all -- but rather a series of small habit changes that accumulated.

I had to change my habits in different **environments**:

1. At home
2. At cafes

I went through a few **phases**:

1. Less Coffee
2. Switching to Decaf Coffee
3. Switching to Herbal Tea Lattes
4. Switching out soda brands

## Phase 1: Less Coffee

My headaches had a huge uptick right around when I started buying half-gallons of caramel iced latte from the grocery store. It was so easy and delicious, I drank a LOT of these!

### At home: no more half-gallons

I didn't just quit - I changed my drinking habit to drinking **homemade cold brew french press iced lattes**.

I would make cold brew french press coffee, and use homemade simple syrup on hand.
It's really just coffee grounds in a french press, in a fridge overnight. I'd plunge it in the morning, and my coffee would be ready!

This was a helpful swap-out for a few reasons:
* **Fewer drinks. **This was less convenient (I had to make it!), and that reduced my consumption a lot.
* **Less caffeine/sugar. **I'm sure it also had a LOT less caffeine and sugar than the store-bought one.
* And I liked it better really, too! The store-bought one was SO sweet that I would hit a sugar limit sometimes.

#### Recipe: Cold brew iced latte

1. (optionally) grind some nice whole-bean coffee
2. Fill the french press 1/6 of the way with coffee grounds
3. Fill the french press the rest of the way with filtered cold water, and put it in the fridge.
4. The next morning, it's ready!
5. Add milk and (optionally) simple syrup.


### At a cafe: smaller coffees

When out at a coffeeshop working, I also started getting smaller coffee drinks.
Small coffee instead of a large coffee.

I was still thirsty for a large! But half of it could be water. I started carrying a water bottle more reliably, or buying water if I forgot.

## Phase 2: Switching to decaf

Once I had LESS coffee, I already had started to have fewer headaches! But unfortunately, still a lot of headaches.

Next was switching to decaf.

### At home: decaf cold brew

Next, I switched to decaf cold brew at home. I would get some nice, quality whole-bean decaf coffee and grind it at home myself. Then I'd use those decaf grinds in my french press in the fridge overnight. Voila

My **homemade decaf cold brew iced lattes** was VERY similar to my previous habit -- the same preparation, and the same delicious flavor -- but now with a lot less caffeine!

Decaf does NOT mean caffeine-free. Estimates vary widely, but I would estimate that the decaf coffee I had was about 1/4 of a similar full-caff.

* Coffee = 1 amount of caffeine
* Decaf coffee = ~1/4 the amount of caffeine
* Caffeine-free = 0 caffeine

Why cold brew?

Cold brew often implies higher caffeine, but that's not how I was using it. The term "cold brew" really just means it is "brewed" with cold water instead of hot water. Since my goal was iced lattes, it was a convenient way to get cold coffee.

#### Recipe: DECAF cold brew iced latte

1. (optionally) grind some nice **decaf** whole-bean coffee (or)
2. Fill the french press 1/6 of the way with coffee grounds
3. Fill the french press the rest of the way with filtered cold water, and put it in the fridge.
4. The next morning, it's ready!
5. Add milk and (optionally) simple syrup.


### At cafes: decaf drinks

At cafes, I started ordering a decaf coffee or a decaf latte. I could even have larges again, since it was less caffeine!

If no decaf options were available, I could still get a caffeinated drink, just a small one.

But I would track which cafes had good decaf options or not, and that changed where I would go.

## Phase 3: Switching to herbal tea lattes

Next up, I quit coffee - but not other forms of caffeine.

### At home: Roiboos Herbal Tea Lattes

I started getting instead
I really like Roiboos - it's definitely not coffee, but it's the closest in flavor of all of the herbal teas. Roiboos is an herbal tea, nicknames "African Red Bush" tea.

When I was visiting a friend in Malaysia a few years ago, we went to a cafe that even offered Roiboos espresso lattes. These were so good!

"Espresso" here means it's prepared through heat and pressure, through an espresso machine. Most people think "espresso = coffee," but this "roisboos espresso" was 100% caffeine free.

My trick to make Roiboos good is to make it STRONG. I add a LOT of roiboos leaves to my french press (with hot water) to get a similar amount of flavor. I add literally triple the amount of roiboos tea leaves.

#### Recipe: Roiboos iced latte

1. Fill a french press 1/3 with roiboos leaves
2. Fill the french press the rest of the way with near-boiling water
3. After 10 minutes (or when it's cooled, or whenever you remember), plunge the french press and put it in the fridge.
4. The next morning, it's ready!
5. Add milk and (optionally) simple syrup.


### At cafes: London Fog with Herbal Tea

I still go to cafes, even though I don't drink coffee!

Unfortunately, most places don't have my favorite, Roiboos. And if they do, many don't make it as strong as I like at home.

Now I order a "London Fog". This is a tea latte with Earl Gray tea (black tea), lavender syrup, and vanilla syrup. It's definitely a latte, and it's pretty good!!

BUT since I'm going for NO caffeine, I ask them "could you make it with an herbal tea, like chamomile?". So far 4/4 of the cafes I've visited have been able to accommodate this, it's very widely available!

## Phase 4: Switching out soda brands

My next caffeine culprit was sodas with caffeine.

Similar to coffee, I started by drinking LESS soda - the mini cans at home, or splitting a soda with someone.

I cut out Pepsi, Coke, and Dr. Pepper.

Now I drink caffeine-free options like Root Beer, 7-Up and Sprite.

Root beer is tricky! Some root beers do have caffeine. My favorite mnemonic is "Barq's has bite" -- Barq's brand root beer has caffeine.

## Reducing chocolate

I used to keep dark chocolate covered almonds on my office desk.

One day well into my caffeine-reduction journey, I ate FIFTEEN of these in a row, and I couldn't sleep at all that night! Whoops!

It turns out dark chocolate does have a lot of caffeine -- at LEAST as much as "a decaf coffee" would have affected me.

I haven't sworn off chocolate completely -- it can be an ingredient in things (like a granola bar with chocolate chips). But I don't keep dark chocolate on my desk anymore, super accessible!

# Curious to reduce your caffeine intake too?

If you want to reduce your caffeine intake, it isn't too hard!

IF you approach it the right way.

## Small habit changes.

A "habit change" here means you have a new "default choice" and you don't have to ACTIVELY choose the options when you're there.

In the process of starting a habit, it's (by definition) NOT a habit yet. It can take many tries working on "habit in progress" before it turns into a "default-choice" habit. That's part of the process!

**For you: What's the first habit-changing small step you could make?**

## Positive re-framing

I tried NOT to focus on "giving up something I like a lot" but INSTEAD focus on "I am going after choices I really like!"

I wanted fewer headaches!
I wanted more energy throughout the day (not just when I was drinking coffee)!
I wanted to make less coffee detours!

I wanted these things -- that was my motivation.

And I was willing to work through making small habit changes to get there.

**For you: What's your why? What's motivating you?**
